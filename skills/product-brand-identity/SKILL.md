# Product brand identity

## What this skill is

Product branding: the bespoke identity of a digital product, designed together with the product. It is not corporate identity consultancy (positioning decks, logo systems for a holding company). The output is a product that is unmistakably itself on every screen while staying fully usable.

This skill works on top of product-design-foundations (the technical floor, with values from Material Design 3 and Apple HIG) and product-ui-patterns (the pattern library the identity is applied to), and is checked by agency-grade-review. Where the client already has a brand, this skill translates it; where the product is new, this skill creates the identity. Decisions made while applying it are captured by design-decision-log.

## What the agencies' screens actually do

Observed in the product screenshots and videos of their published case studies (not their stated philosophy). Take the principle, never copy the look.

- Work & Co (Aesop, Gatorade Gx, Epic Games): canvas is warm cream or near-black, never grey. Colour is almost absent inside the UI: Aesop has no accent, Gatorade keeps its gradient for one day card and one celebratory number, Epic uses blue only on a discount badge. Big confident headlines, small meta, hairline rows instead of cards, one full-width primary action at the bottom (white pill on black, black bar on cream). The core flow is never sacrificed to the brand.
- MetaLab (Uber, Uber Eats, Headspace, Slack): black and white with one accent (Uber blue for progress and the add action, green for Eats), white sheets with a grabber and radius around 16 to 20, hairline rows, grey pill secondaries, the ETA set huge with a small am/pm. Headspace: cream canvas, friendly geometric grotesk, brand colours as a categorical set for content categories and orange as the single primary accent. Personality in colour, copy and interaction, in categories that are usually grey.
- Clay (STC Bank, Snapchat, Joe & The Juice, Eden): one colour logic per product (purple identity plus green action; camera-first white chrome; pastel blocks plus pink action) and one typographic voice (rounded geometric; condensed caps; serif pairing). Custom duotone icons, one colour per category chip, radius held constant per product (16 to 20 for STC, 4 for Joe & The Juice). Component craft is conventional and clean; brand is carried by colour, type, icons and copy, not by layout tricks.
- Fantasy (Fubo, Priceline Penny, Harrods, Vimeo): dark charcoal with one hot accent where focus is a fill (Fubo); an AI persona whose gradient encodes state and a brand sheet with named gradients and a token table (Penny); serif display with italic, editorial photography and one deep green action colour for luxury commerce (Harrods). Brand as product infrastructure, including motion states.
- AKQA (Louis Vuitton app, Montblanc Digital Paper): the app reads like a magazine. Full-bleed editorial photography, minimal white chrome, no accent at all (imagery is the colour), text-only navigation with a tiny active marker. Montblanc is a monochrome paper system with thin type. Luxury is restraint plus imagery plus pacing.
- Framna (Brand New Day, NPO Zapp, PostNord): one strong colour decision per app (a teal-to-blue gradient as the canvas of the money screens; neon green on black for kids TV), big light-weight amounts, frosted stat cards, white detail sheets, a brand serif for screen titles and a neutral sans for data. Nordic functional clarity on native structure.
- Wolff Olins (Uber, Instacart, TikTok): identity as a small system of shared assets (one typeface with tight tracking, one colour stance, one device such as a white frame or a carrot-leaf arrow) that product and marketing share; the brand idea often comes from a product behaviour ("tap tap nom nom" is the add-to-cart interaction). Coherent rather than consistent, able to disappear when in the way.

## Principles

1. Brand and product are one design. Decide identity and interface together. Never skin a finished UI.
2. One brand idea drives every carrier. If the idea cannot be said in one sentence, the identity will fragment.
3. Distinctive through few decisive choices: one signature typeface or pairing, one colour stance, one shape language, one motion signature. Everything else stays quiet. Three bold choices with discipline beat ten half-choices.
4. Coherent, not uniform. The product feels like one author across surfaces, with brand intensity varying by moment.
5. Brand serves the job. Where the user works (forms, tables, settings, payment), brand steps back into typography, spacing and tone. Where the user pauses (onboarding, empty states, success, app icon, marketing), brand steps forward.
6. Break one category convention on purpose and keep the rest. Users must recognise the category; the brand must be recognisable within it.
7. Identity has to survive production: screen-tested type, contrast-safe colour, token-ready values, motion that holds 60fps on a mid-range Android phone.
8. Intensity, not volume. A bespoke identity pushes one or two carriers to an extreme (an enormous light amount, a total absence of accent, a focus state that floods with colour, condensed caps everywhere) and keeps every other decision on the platform defaults. Everything at medium is generic; everything loud is noise.

## The ten decisions that make a product identity

Make these in order, each in one line, before drawing anything. Together they are the identity; everything else is execution.

1. Canvas: light or dark comes from the scene of use first (who, where, in what light: a courier outdoors at noon, a trader in a dim room, a child on the sofa after dinner), then the tone from the product: warm cream (lifestyle, editorial, wellbeing), near-black (sport, gaming, entertainment, luxury-tech), white (utility, luxury commerce, assistants), or the brand gradient on hero screens only (finance brands with a strong hue). Never a mid-grey canvas, and never warm off-white because it looks tasteful.
2. Accent economy: one accent for action, selection, live status and progress, covering a small fraction of the screen (roughly 5 percent or less outside brand moments). Zero accent is a valid luxury or editorial choice. A categorical set only when the content has categories.
3. Type voice: a display voice (bold geometric, condensed caps, serif with italic, rounded, light) for headlines and hero figures, plus a neutral workhorse for UI text. Decide size contrast (dramatic or even), case, tracking and weight; these matter more than the font name.
4. Hero figure: the one number or headline per screen set 2 to 3 times the body size with its unit or label small beside it, and a quiet screen around it.
5. Separation method: spacing first, hairlines second, tints third, borders fourth, shadows last. Cards are single-purpose (sheet, tile, stat card) and never nested.
6. Radius: one logic per product, held constant, from the Material 3 shape scale (0, 4, 8, 12, 16, 28, full). Sharp: 0 to 4 (luxury, editorial, playful-sharp). Friendly: 8 to 12. Soft: 16 to 28 for cards and sheets, with pills for chips and primary buttons. Buttons and cards share the logic.
7. Imagery role: imagery is the colour (chrome goes monochrome, art direction rules become part of the system) or imagery is content inside a coloured system. Text over images gets a gradient scrim that fades into the canvas.
8. Navigation and action chrome: native and restrained. 3 to 5 bottom tabs with tiny labels or icons only, a minimal header, sheets with grabbers, one full-width primary action at the bottom of task screens, secondaries as grey pills or hairline outlines.
9. Brand moments: launch, login and onboarding on the full brand canvas; success with the accent or gradient; empty states with illustration; working screens monochrome.
10. Signature: one recurring brand device (a frame, an arrow, a shape family, an orb) and one recurring motion move (state-encoding colour, tap-to-add, focus fill, a launch burst), both tied to the brand idea, with everything else fast and plain.

## Process

### 1. Brand brief (one page, before any visuals)

- Product job and audience, one line each.
- Brand idea: one sentence, the organising thought. Examples of the form: "a calm desk for chaotic money", "a confetti cannon in a grey office", "a Nordic post office that shows you the parcel".
- Personality: 3 attributes plus 1 tension. Each attribute gets a "this, not that" line (warm, not cute; precise, not cold). The tension is what makes it specific (playful but never silly).
- Category conventions: list 5. Mark the one you will break and why. Keep the other 4.
- References: 5 to 8, each with one sentence on what to take from it. Never "make it like X".
- Audience world: five to seven things this audience knows by heart (a document, a tool, a place, a ritual, a publication, a notation, a signage or identity system), each with one line on why it fits what the product does and one on how it looks (ground and main colours, type voices, the one mark that makes it recognisable). Then name the rut: the page this category always ships and its obvious opposite. Neither is a direction.
- Constraints: existing brand assets, platforms, accessibility, markets and languages, tech stack, imagery budget.

### 2. Directions (2 or 3, deliberately different)

Each direction contains: type choice, colour stance, shape language, one hero screen, one dense working screen, one loud brand moment (launch, onboarding or success), the app icon or logo lockup, and 3 lines of copy in its voice. Name each direction after its idea, not after its look. Judge directions on the dense screen as much as on the hero; a direction that only works on the hero is a poster, not a product identity.

Method, so the directions are actually different: write the decisions as a matrix (one column per direction; twelve rows: the ten decisions, the carrier pushed to its extreme, the convention broken). Directions must differ in at least three decisions, must each push a different carrier to its extreme (one pushes type, one pushes canvas or colour, one pushes imagery or motion), and must each pick a different convention to break. Pull options from the catalogues below, and take at least one direction's device, canvas or type voice from the audience world, not from the catalogues. A brief that brings its own picture (a product name, a metaphor) gets at most one direction built on the literal reading. Each direction names its honest risk in one line. If two columns read the same at a glance, one of them is not a direction.

Beside the directions, always offer the category standard played straight: name two or three products it should sit beside, match their craft level, add no quirk. It is the safe choice made on purpose and executed fully, never a diluted direction.

### 3. System

Write the ten decisions above for the chosen direction, then turn them into the carriers below, then into tokens (primitive and semantic, light and dark) and component expression rules. Document do and don't with real screens.

### 4. Apply and test

Apply across the key flows, including the ugly ones (settings, errors, long lists). Run the review tests at the end of this file. Fix, then document.

## Brand carriers in a product

### Typography (the primary carrier)

- Choose for character and performance: full character set for the markets served, tabular figures, good rendering at 14px, variable weights, a licence that covers app and web. Name the face with its reason; the spent faces are listed in typography-craft.
- Pairing strategies: (a) one versatile grotesk doing everything through size and weight; (b) an expressive display face (serif, slab, geometric, humanist) for headlines plus a neutral workhorse for UI; (c) a mono accent for data-heavy or technical products. Never more than 2 families plus an optional mono.
- The display face appears only at display sizes and in brand moments. UI text always uses the workhorse.
- Define the typographic voice beyond the font name: size contrast (dramatic or even), case convention, tracking, leading feel, alignment habit. Two products can share a typeface and feel completely different through these.

### Colour (the stance)

- Pick one stance per product: neutral canvas plus one accent; saturated brand colour or gradient as the canvas of hero screens; dark-first with one luminous accent; warm paper-like palette; duotone; or monochrome with imagery as the colour.
- Keep the accent scarce: action, selection, live status and progress only, roughly 5 percent of the screen or less outside brand moments. If the accent appears on every element, the product has no accent.
- Derive the whole system from the brand hues: tinted neutrals, the accent scale, and semantic colours harmonised with the brand (a brand red and an error red must not clash, so one of them moves).
- Write the signature colour rule: exactly where the accent appears (primary action, selection, focus, one brand surface) and where it never appears.
- Fix contrast at system level: create UI-safe variants of brand colours (text-safe, surface-safe) and document that the marketing hex and the UI token differ on purpose.
- Light and dark are both designed from the brand, never inverted. A brand-colour canvas turns near-black in dark mode and the brand colour moves to actions, shapes and the hero figure; text on a fixed brand or image ground keeps its own on-image colours in both themes.

### Shape and surface

- Radius language from the Material 3 shape scale (0, 4, 8, 12, 16, 28, full): sharp 0 to 4 (luxury, editorial, playful-sharp), friendly 8 to 12, soft 16 to 28 for cards and sheets, pills for chips and primary buttons where the brand is friendly. One value per component tier (controls, containers, media) with consistent ratios between tiers, held constant across the product.
- Separation method: spacing first, hairlines second, tints third, borders fourth, shadows last. Single-purpose cards only; never a card inside a card.
- Density and whitespace are brand too: generous and editorial, or compact and tool-like. Decide, then keep it.

### Iconography

- One family whose stroke and geometry match the typeface (icon stroke weight visually equals text stroke weight at the same size). Custom icons only for the 5 to 10 brand-specific objects; everything else from one consistent set.
- Decide outline or filled, corner style and optical sizes. The app icon follows the same geometry.

### Imagery, illustration and 3D

- Decide the one approach: photography (with art direction rules for light, crop, colour treatment, people versus objects), illustration (style, line, palette subset, level of abstraction), 3D (materials, lighting, camera) or none (type-led).
- Write the art direction as rules someone else can follow and brief an artist or a generative tool with.
- Imagery never competes with content. In product UI it lives in brand moments; in commerce the product imagery is the hero and the brand recedes around it.
- When no imagery exists yet, draw or generate stand-ins in the chosen art direction and write the brief beside them (subject, light, ground, crop). Never grey boxes, never stock, always marked as stand-ins.
- When imagery is the colour (fashion, food, games, media), the chrome goes monochrome: white or black text, hairlines, no accent fills. Text over images sits on a gradient scrim that fades the image into the canvas, never on an unscrimmed photo.

### Motion signature

- Define three things: the easing family (crisp, fluid or springy), the base durations, and one signature move that recurs (a reveal, a morph, a stagger, a transition). Observed signatures: a persona whose colour encodes its state, an add-to-cart tap that is also the brand line, a focus state that fills with the accent, product cut-outs drifting as a pattern, a colour burst on launch.
- Motion still obeys the product baseline: UI motion under 300ms, interruptible, purposeful. Brand sets the character; it never buys slowness. A signature that needs blur, a mask or a colour change is built from static layers (a pre-blurred copy, a clip) so only transform and opacity animate; it plays at brand moments and after an event, once, never on frequently seen data.
- Launch moment: a short launch animation (under 1 second) that resolves into the first screen. The same idea reappears, smaller, in loading and success states.
- When Emil Kowalski's skills are installed, use them to build and review the motion.

### Sound and haptics (mobile)

- Haptics for meaningful confirmations only, mapped to a small vocabulary (success, warning, selection). Sound off by default unless it is core to the product.

### Voice and tone

- Voice: the 3 attributes written as rules with examples per surface: button labels, empty states, errors, notifications, onboarding, marketing. Include a "never say" list.
- Tone shifts by moment: warmest in onboarding and success, plainest in errors, money and permissions.
- Personality lives in the small moments (empty states, confirmations, loading copy), not in every label.

### Brand device

- One graphic element derived from the brand idea that recurs across product and marketing and that no competitor has: a frame, an arrow, a shape family, a halftone, a glitch, an orb, a pattern of the product itself. It is the most recognisable bespoke element in agency work and the one most often missing from generic products.
- Define it once (construction, proportions, colour behaviour, motion if any) and give it 3 to 5 named places: launch, empty states, section openers, marketing heroes, the app icon. Outside those places it does not appear.
- The device may become the icon geometry, the loading indicator or the signature motion, but it is never wallpaper behind content.

### Logo and lockups in product

- The logo appears in the app icon, launch screen, about and auth screens, and marketing. It does not sit in every screen header unless the platform convention expects it (web top-left home link).
- Never stretch brand elements into UI decoration: logo shapes as backgrounds, brand pattern on every surface, mascots in forms.

### Convention breaks observed

- Text-only bottom navigation with a tiny marker (LV). Zero accent colour in a commerce product (Aesop). Gradient text for a success number (Gatorade). Focus as a flood fill instead of an outline (Fubo). Emoji in programme titles and neon numerals (NPO Zapp). The assistant's persona doubling as its status indicator (Penny). Uppercase for every label in a food app (Joe & The Juice). A serif for screen titles inside a banking app (Brand New Day). The brand line as the add-to-cart interaction (Instacart).
- Each break is one per product, sits on top of otherwise native structure, and is traceable to the brand idea.

## Brand moments map

For every product, mark where brand is loud, medium or quiet:

- Loud: app icon, launch and login (full brand canvas), onboarding, first empty state (illustration), major success (accent or gradient on the hero figure), marketing site, notification tone.
- Medium: navigation, home, dashboards, cards, section headers.
- Quiet: forms, tables, settings, payment and checkout, cart, library and list views, errors, dense data, permissions. These go monochrome: type, spacing, hairlines and tone only.
- Reading surfaces (articles, help, documentation, changelog): the identity owns the frame (masthead, navigation, ground, title and section openers, the setting of code, tables and callouts) and the column stays calm: the reading face, the measure, nothing behind the text, navigation nobody has to learn.

Rule: a user working in a quiet zone should be able to forget the brand and still feel it in the type, spacing and tone.

## Translating an existing corporate brand into product

- Keep: the idea, the type family (or its licensed screen companion), the primary hues, the voice.
- Adapt: UI-safe colour variants, a UI type scale, an icon set matched to the type, motion character, component expression. Document each adaptation and why.
- Protect: the usability floor from product-design-foundations. If a brand colour fails contrast as text, it becomes a surface or accent colour instead, and this is proposed to the brand owner with the measured ratios.
- Extend: most brand guidelines have no rules for product. Write the product brand sheet and offer it back as an addition to the guidelines.

## Deliverables

- Product brand sheet (1 to 2 pages): idea, attributes, type, colour stance, shape, icons, imagery, motion signature, voice, brand moments map.
- Tokens derived from the brand (primitive and semantic), light and dark.
- Component expression rules: how buttons, inputs, cards, navigation and empty states express the brand.
- Motion signature spec: easing, durations, the signature move, launch animation.
- Voice guide with examples per surface.
- Do and don't gallery on real screens.
- Production asset list: icon set, illustration kit, app icon variants, imagery treatments.

## Anti-patterns

- Brand as decoration: logo everywhere, brand colour on every element, brand pattern behind content.
- Identity that is only a logo: swap the logo and nothing changes.
- Trend as identity: default purple gradients, glassmorphism, 3D blobs, grainy gradients, "AI aesthetic" glows, and the generated looks of the moment (warm cream with a serif display and a terracotta accent; near-black with one neon accent; hairlines with an italic serif and small tracked labels; flat ink with hard offset shadows). A trend is allowed only as the deliberate convention-break, and only if it will age acceptably; a generated look only when the brief asks for exactly that.
- Generic SaaS as identity: Inter, blue 600, 8px radius, cards with 1px borders, hero plus three feature tiles. Acceptable as a baseline, never as an identity.
- A mid-grey canvas, or a canvas chosen by default rather than as the first brand decision.
- Accent spread over the screen (filled tab bars, coloured headers, coloured cards and buttons all at once).
- Nested cards, bordered media grids, shadows on everything.
- Too many choices: 3 typefaces, 4 accents, mixed radii, mixed icon sets.
- Loud brand in quiet zones: illustrations inside forms, animated logos in checkout, jokes in error states.
- Voice that changes personality between screens.
- Imagery from a different universe than the UI (stock photos next to geometric illustration).
- Marketing hex values used as UI text colours despite failing contrast.

## Review: does the product feel like itself?

- Ten decisions test: can each of the ten decisions be read off the screens in one line? A decision that cannot be named was never made.
- Default count test: count the ten decisions sitting on the generic default (white canvas, blue accent, neutral grotesk at an even scale, no hero figure, cards, radius 8, no imagery role, standard chrome, no brand moments, no signature). A default the brief gives a reason for (white for luxury commerce) counts as a decision. More than six defaults means there is no identity. Agency-grade products have at least four non-default decisions, each traceable to the brand idea.
- Intensity test: name the one or two carriers pushed to an extreme. None means the identity is timid; more than two means it is noise.
- Rut test: is this the page the category always ships, its obvious opposite, or one of the generated looks? Then every part of it needs its reason from the brief.
- Memory test: what would someone describe an hour after seeing one screen? A mood means the identity has not committed; a thing (the hatching, the plates, the flip) means it has.
- Device test: is there one recurring brand device, defined once and used in named places? A product with no device, or with a device used as wallpaper, is a finding.
- Accent coverage test: estimate the share of a working screen covered by the accent. Above roughly 5 percent outside a brand moment is a finding.
- Swap test: replace the logo with a competitor's. If the screens still look right, there is no identity.
- Squint test: blurred, is the hierarchy still clear and the colour stance recognisable?
- One-author test: put 6 screens side by side (hero, list, form, empty, error, settings). Same type voice, spacing, radius, icon stroke and tone?
- Category test: show a dense screen to someone unfamiliar. Can they name the category in 3 seconds and two of the brand attributes in 10?
- Moments test: are loud moments actually loud and quiet zones actually quiet?
- Production test: contrast passes, fonts load with matched fallbacks, motion runs on a mid-range Android device, dark mode designed.
- Idea test: can every major choice be traced to the brand idea in one sentence?

When reviewing, output a table with Finding, Where, Why it matters and Fix, ordered by impact.
