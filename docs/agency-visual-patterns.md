# Agency visual patterns: what the screens actually do

Observations from the product screenshots and videos in the public case studies of Work & Co, MetaLab, Clay, Fantasy, AKQA, Framna and Wolff Olins (18 case studies, roughly 95 frames, reviewed November 2026). These are observations of published work, written as checkable patterns, not the agencies' own rules. The four skills in this repo encode the conclusions; this document is the evidence.

## Method

Each case study page was opened in a browser, the product images and videos were viewed at source resolution, and the following were noted per product: canvas, colour economy, typography, hierarchy, spacing and separation, radius, components, navigation, imagery, motion, copy, and how the agency presents the work. Agencies were chosen for the range of product branding they practise, from functional Nordic apps to luxury commerce.

## Per agency

### Work & Co (Aesop, Gatorade Gx, Epic Games Store and Party Hub)

- Canvas is warm cream or near-black, never grey. Aesop: cream with a dark cart drawer. Gatorade and Epic: black.
- Colour is almost absent inside the UI. Aesop has no accent. Gatorade reserves its orange-to-magenta gradient for one day card and one celebratory number. Epic uses blue only for a discount badge.
- Big confident headlines and small meta. Gatorade: bold grotesk around 28 to 32pt top-left on photography that fades into black. Aesop: light headlines around 24 to 26pt on mobile, hairline label and value rows on desktop.
- One full-width primary action at the bottom: a white pill on black (Gatorade), a black bar on cream (Aesop).
- Separation by hairlines and spacing. Epic's store grid has no borders at all; metadata sits under each tile in grey.
- Navigation: bottom tab bar with 4 items and tiny uppercase labels (Gatorade); minimal header with wordmark, dot and hamburger (Aesop mobile web).
- The game's own typography (condensed uppercase) appears only on Fortnite-facing Party Hub surfaces; the store itself stays neutral so the game art is the hero.
- Presentation: flat device frames on flat fields (pure black, warm sand, pale grey-blue) in 3-up or 4-up rows; one angled 3D phone for a hero; brand guideline pages shown as hairline-gridded spec documents.

### MetaLab (Uber, Uber Eats, Headspace)

- Uber: black and white with one blue accent for progress and the add action; Uber Eats swaps in green. Bottom sheets with a grabber and radius around 16 to 20pt; hairline rows; grey pill secondary buttons ("Change", "Remove"); circular icon buttons with labels beneath; ETA set very large (around 32pt) with a small am/pm and a right-aligned grey label; black route line and a black pill status tag on the map.
- Headspace: cream canvas, dark grey text, friendly geometric grotesk; brand colours used as a categorical set for content categories (orange, purple, blue, yellow, green, pink pills with radius around 10 to 12); orange as the single primary accent; quote cards with an oversized orange quote glyph, large quote text around 28px, small grey attribution.
- Presentation: lifestyle photography of the product in hand, extreme close-ups of a single component on a light grey field, 3D angled devices on textured backgrounds, short loops.

### Clay (STC Bank, Snapchat, Joe & The Juice)

- STC Bank: purple as identity, green as the action colour (primary buttons and links), dark navy canvas in dark mode, lavender-tinted neutrals in light mode. Balance around 32pt bold with a small unit; segmented pill tabs; cards with radius 16 to 20 and square icon buttons with radius 12; transaction rows with avatar, title, subtitle, right-aligned amount and relative time; 5-item tab bar; custom duotone icons (purple stroke, green fill) and one colour per category chip.
- Snapchat AR shopping: minimal white chrome over the camera; product name and price centred; two pills (white primary, translucent secondary); brand yellow only in the tab highlight and the presentation backdrop.
- Joe & The Juice: bold condensed uppercase type, pastel colour blocks, product cut-outs on pastel grounds as repeating patterns, white cards with a small 4px radius, pink as the only action colour, playful copy ("A juicer is making your order").
- Presentation: hand-held lifestyle shots, phones on flat brand-colour fields, component close-ups floating over photography, icon sheets on light panels, UI element collections.

### Fantasy (Fubo, Priceline Penny, Harrods)

- Fubo (10-foot TV UI): dark charcoal, one orange-red accent for LIVE tags and the focused card (focus is a fill, not an outline), bold condensed uppercase event headlines with team logos, rows of 16:9 cards, schedule list with a date rail, multiview with a contextual hint.
- Penny (AI assistant): the brand is a glossy sphere whose mesh gradient encodes state (blue idle, cyan listening, magenta talking, orange generating); white canvas with a faint blue tint, frosted panels, rounded cards around 16, pill chips, a persistent "Ask Penny" pill input; the case study shows the brand sheet with a type sample, named gradients and a token table.
- Harrods: white canvas, Harrods serif with italic for display, grotesk for UI, deep green as the action colour and gold wordmark, editorial magazine layouts with photography overlapping headlines, minimal product cards.
- Presentation: cinematic 3D rooms for TV, isometric grids of floating phones on gradients, tablets on brand-colour gradients, grids of many page types on black.

### AKQA (Louis Vuitton app, Montblanc Digital Paper)

- LV: the app reads like a magazine. Full-bleed editorial photography, minimal white chrome in a geometric sans, no accent colour (the imagery is the colour), small white product panels overlapping photos, text-only bottom navigation with a tiny marker for the active tab, outlined circular play and chevron buttons.
- Montblanc: a monochrome system (black, a ramp of greys, paper white), thin type, paper and pen metaphors, frosted panels over handwriting, slow soft motion.
- Presentation: phones in an overlapping grid bleeding off black, short films with a single caption to the right, a bento mosaic of system elements (logo construction, type specimen, icon on a grid with dimensions, colour ramp, components).

### Framna (Brand New Day, NPO Zapp)

- Brand New Day (pension and investing): a teal-to-blue brand gradient as the canvas of the money screens, white type with the amount very large in a light weight ("€ 186.663"), frosted translucent stat cards, white detail sheets with radius around 20, underlined tab selectors, two-series line chart, a brand serif for screen titles and a humanist sans for everything else, friendly Dutch copy.
- NPO Zapp (kids TV): dark canvas, neon green accent and a purple glow, 16:9 thumbnail cards with small radius, bold rounded titles, emoji allowed, a Top 10 rail with neon-outlined numerals, dark pill search, 3-item icon-only tab bar.
- Presentation: flat phones on pale tinted fields in 3-up rows for finance; angled 3D phone on dark with neon brand shapes for entertainment. The presentation style follows the product's personality.

### Wolff Olins (Uber, TikTok, Instacart)

- Uber: black and white identity, blue reserved for the single CTA, bold geometric grotesk with tight tracking, photography inside a white frame with the wordmark in the margin, a device that works on billboards, email and product cards. Product email: big headline, blue button, ride list with icon, name, meta and right-aligned price.
- TikTok: identity built from the product's own UI elements (the heart, the glitch offset) blown up as brand assets.
- Instacart: cream canvas, deep green type, carrot orange, huge tightly tracked headlines, the carrot-leaf arrow reused as an icon, copy that doubles as the interaction idea ("tap tap nom nom").

## Cross-agency patterns

These hold across most of the 18 case studies and are encoded as rules in the skills.

1. The canvas is the first brand decision. Warm cream (lifestyle, editorial, wellbeing), near-black (sport, gaming, entertainment, luxury-tech), white (utility, luxury commerce, assistants), or the brand gradient for hero screens (finance brands with a strong hue). None use a mid-grey canvas.
2. Colour economy. One accent, used for action, selection, live status or progress, covering a small fraction of the screen. Luxury and editorial products use no accent. A categorical colour set appears only when the content has categories.
3. Type carries identity. A display voice (bold geometric, condensed caps, serif with italic, rounded, light) for headlines and hero numbers, plus a neutral workhorse for UI text. Two products can share a typeface and feel different through size contrast, case, tracking and weight.
4. The hero figure. The one number or headline that matters on a screen is set 2 to 3 times the body size, with its unit or label small beside it. The rest of the screen stays quiet so the figure reads instantly.
5. Separation by spacing and hairlines, not boxes. Cards are single-purpose (a sheet, a product tile, a stat card) and never nested; grids of media tiles have no borders.
6. Radius is a brand decision and is constant per product: 0 to 2 (luxury, editorial), 4 (sharp and playful), 10 to 12 (friendly), 16 to 20 (soft consumer, sheets). Buttons inside a product share one radius logic with its cards.
7. Photography is the colour in imagery-led products. The chrome goes monochrome and the art direction (light, crop, treatment) becomes part of the system. Text over images sits on a gradient scrim that fades into the canvas.
8. Navigation is native and restrained. 3 to 5 bottom tabs with tiny labels or icons only, minimal headers (wordmark or title plus one or two icon buttons), bottom sheets with grabbers, no hamburger menus in native apps.
9. One full-width primary action at the bottom of a task screen; secondaries as grey pills or hairline outlines; destructive and secondary actions never compete with it.
10. Brand moments are placed deliberately. Launch, login and onboarding use the full brand canvas; success uses the accent or gradient; empty states use illustration; working screens (tables, cart, library, settings) go monochrome.
11. Motion signature is one recurring move tied to the brand idea: state-encoding colour (Penny), tap-to-add (Instacart), focus fill (Fubo), product patterns (Joe & The Juice), a colour burst on launch (Slack).
12. Copy puts personality in small moments and keeps controls plain: "tap tap nom nom" and "Where to, Jessica?" next to "Add money", "Change", "Choose room".
13. Dark mode is designed: canvas between #0A0A0A and #121212 (or a brand-tinted dark such as navy), surfaces a few steps lighter, secondary text around 60 to 70 percent white, no shadows, the accent on one element.
14. Dense screens exist and are shown. Every agency presents the list, the table, the cart or the library alongside the hero.

## Presentation conventions

How the agencies show product work, which also sets the bar for a designer's own case studies and client decks.

- Flat device frames on flat colour fields, in 3-up or 4-up rows. The field follows the product's personality: black for sport and gaming, cream or pale tint for lifestyle and finance, the brand colour for identity work.
- One angled 3D device for the hero, then flat frames for everything else. 3D is rationed.
- Component close-ups: a single component cropped huge on a neutral field (a fare-split card, a category chip set, a stat card over a lifestyle photo).
- System sheets: a type sample, colours or gradients with names, a token table, an icon sheet, or a bento mosaic of elements with construction lines.
- Context photography: the product in a hand, on a table, in a living room, so scale and use are visible.
- Short captioned loops (3 to 10 seconds) for motion and interaction, with one line of caption.
- Before and after where a redesign exists; outcomes in numbers where known.
- Dense and ugly screens included, not only heroes.
